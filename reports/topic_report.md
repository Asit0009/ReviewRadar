# Topic Report

Generated by `python -m reviewpulse.nlp.topic_report` from `artifacts/nmf_topics/`, the processed reviews and `reports/topic_experiments.json`.

## 1. What was done

Topics are found with **TF-IDF + NMF**. TF-IDF turns each review into a list of word weights, where words that are common in every review count less. NMF (non-negative matrix factorization) then splits that table into 20 topics, each a set of words that tend to appear together, and says how much of each topic every review contains. Each review is tagged with the topic it contains most of.

- 29,946 reviews, **28,702 tagged** (95.8%). The other 1,244 contain none of the 8,000 words and phrases the model knows, so they have no topic.
- Words and phrases of 1 to 2 words, kept if they appear in at least 20 reviews and in at most 50% of them; the 8,000 most frequent are used.
- Stop words: standard English, plus words that appear in every review ("product", "bought") and words that only praise ("excellent", "thank").
- The topics were learned from the 23,576 reviews with at least 8 words, then applied to all reviews. Very short reviews carry almost no topic information.
- Percentages below are shares of the tagged reviews, the same way the dashboard reports them.

## 2. Why these settings (comparison of five setups)

Each setup was fitted with 3 random seeds on the same 29,946 reviews; the table shows the mean (and the lowest and highest seed).

| Setup | Topics | Biggest topic | Coherence (NPMI, higher is better) | Distinct top words | Weakly assigned reviews |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| v1: 14 topics, 20,000 features, min_df 5 (first version) | 14 | 34.2% (34.1-34.4) | +0.142 (+0.141 to +0.143) | 0.76 | 31.0% |
| 20 topics, same words as v1 | 20 | 25.2% (24.3-25.9) | +0.127 (+0.123 to +0.131) | 0.72 | 41.3% |
| 30 topics, same words as v1 | 30 | 11.2% (9.8-12.6) | +0.127 (+0.124 to +0.130) | 0.71 | 49.6% |
| 14 topics + extra filler stop words (rejected) | 14 | 41.7% (40.4-42.3) | +0.151 (+0.151 to +0.151) | 0.79 | 26.2% |
| 20 topics + praise stop words + min_df 20 (adopted) | 20 | 24.4% (23.0-25.7) | +0.139 (+0.138 to +0.140) | 0.74 | 40.3% |

How to read it:

- **Biggest topic** is the share of reviews in the largest topic. A very large value means one topic is a catch-all.
- **Coherence** checks whether a topic's top words really appear together in the same reviews. Differences smaller than the seed-to-seed range are not meaningful.
- **Weakly assigned** reviews have a main topic that holds less than half of their topic weight. Many reviews talk about two things at once (price and size), so some weak assignment is normal and goes up when there are more topics.
- More topics always shrink the biggest topic but spread reviews thinner, so the number of topics was chosen to keep topics readable, not to make the biggest one small.
- Adding more filler words to the stop list made the biggest topic larger, so that idea was rejected.
- The saved model uses seed 42. Its biggest topic holds 19.8% of the tagged reviews; the three seeds in the table gave 23.0% to 25.7% for the same settings. So the seed matters, and a single value is approximate.

## 4. The largest topic

**General use and results (mixed products)** holds 5,692 reviews (19.8%). Its reviews come from many categories (Health and Household 20%, Beauty and Personal Care 14%, Electronics 11%), its average strongest-topic share is 0.51 (the other topics average 0.60), and 55% of its reviews are weakly assigned.

A catch-all. Its reviews are about very different products (a solar panel, pencils, a calming supplement, cleaning wipes, an oil) and mostly say how long something was used or how it worked. It exists because many reviews have no strong product or aspect word, so NMF groups the leftover general words together. It is large because that is what the reviews look like, not because it describes one subject.

This was not forced to be smaller. It is the part of the review text that does not fit a clearer theme.

## 6. Limitations

- NMF finds groups of words that appear together. It does not understand meaning, so a topic can mix two ideas (see the age and years topic).
- A review gets only one topic, although many mention several. The topic score says how dominant it was.
- Several topics are about an aspect that applies to any product (price, quality, size, fit) and not about a kind of product. That is useful for a review dashboard but it means topics overlap with product categories only partly.
- The number of topics is a choice. More topics split themes further but make each less clear.
- Alternatives not tried here: LDA (similar idea, probabilistic), BERTopic (embedding-based, needs more memory and time), and keyword rules.
